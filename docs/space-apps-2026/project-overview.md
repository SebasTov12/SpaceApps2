# SAROS | Space Apps 2026 Project Brief

## The challenge

Disasters can harm people and also disrupt the systems that sustain them: homes, roads, farms, livestock, mining, commerce, health services, and supply chains. Satellite measurements are valuable only when they are connected to these local consequences and communicated with their uncertainty intact.

## Our approach

SAROS (Socioeconomic Analysis and Risk Observation System) brings together:

- NASA–ISRO NISAR radar products for observing changes in the land surface.
- Colombian event and landslide records for historical context.
- Geographic, demographic, housing, infrastructure, and economic-exposure layers.
- An explainable relative-priority workflow that shows its data sources and gaps.

The product is a decision-support prototype for screening locations and planning technical review. It is not an emergency authority, an earthquake predictor, or a guarantee that a route or location is safe.

## Intended users

- Municipal risk-management and planning teams.
- Local responders and infrastructure operators.
- Agricultural and community organizations.
- Researchers and judges reviewing an auditable geospatial prototype.

## User journey

1. Explore a Colombian region on the SAROS globe.
2. Select a location and inspect available satellite and territorial evidence.
3. Review historical reported impacts and exposed population or services.
4. Check the data date, source, model mode, and validation status.
5. Use the result to direct a technical inspection or preparedness discussion.

## Project outcomes

SAROS aims to make complex earth-observation data easier to inspect, relate land-change signals to local exposure, and make recommendations traceable. The current verified production capability and its limits are documented in [Validation and Results](validation-and-results.md).

## Design principles

- **Traceable:** source, date, processing, and model version accompany the report.
- **Measurable:** show sample sizes, holdout periods, error metrics, and a baseline.
- **Human-reviewed:** outputs support local technical assessment.
- **Honest about uncertainty:** distinguish catalog assets, observations, screening, backtests, and forecasts.
- **Socioeconomic:** account for people, homes, services, livelihoods, and access.
