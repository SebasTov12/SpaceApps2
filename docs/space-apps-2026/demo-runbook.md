# Space Apps 2026 Demo Runbook

## 1. Introduce the problem

Explain that the same ground movement may affect homes, roads, farms, services, and livelihoods. SAROS combines satellite observations with local exposure context so teams can prioritize review.

## 2. Explore one location

Open the globe, select a Colombian location, and show its available satellite metadata, local exposure layers, and source information. State whether the displayed change layer is exploratory or demo data.

## 3. Show the measured result

Open **Data & validation**. Show the daily snapshot time, the NISAR catalog/raster counts, real LOS observation count, and forecast-run count. The production check on 22 November 2026 found 227 raster assets and 18 GUNW granules, but zero quality-controlled LOS observations and zero stored LOS forecast runs.

Then show the 2025 holdout table. Explain that its MAE evaluates historical reported-impact estimation, not future movement prediction. The rows are compared with a global-mean baseline using 3,315 training and 1,273 holdout records.

## 4. Explain what a result can support

Use a relative priority to motivate field inspection or preparedness discussion. Mention source dates and what is missing. Do not call it a probability, an alert, a prediction of an earthquake, or a safe evacuation route.

## 5. Close with the next validation milestone

Process a consistent NISAR stack into stable-reference LOS observations, compare them with independent geodetic or field measurements, and run spatial-temporal holdouts against a persistence baseline before claiming forecast skill.

## Three-minute script

> SAROS connects NISAR's radar view of land change with the people, infrastructure, and livelihoods exposed in Colombia. We can show where our data and historical impact estimator are measurable today, and we make missing evidence visible. Our impact table is a retrospective 2025 holdout result, not a forecast score. We have NISAR raster assets, but no validated LOS observation series or stored NISAR forecasts yet, so we present the satellite signal as exploratory screening. The next milestone is independent geodetic validation before issuing any forecast claim.
